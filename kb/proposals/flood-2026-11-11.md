## Method

- Per postcode district (outward code, e.g. `EX4`): the share of residential properties in the file at **high** risk (a 1 in 30 (3.3%) or greater chance of flooding in any year).
- Districts with fewer than 50 residential properties are left out; 2,099 districts considered.
- Each tier becomes one factor `{ field: "postcodeDistrict", operator: "in", values: [...] }` in group `flood_risk` (either/or, so the linter checks the tiers don't overlap).

| Factor | Share at risk | Points | Districts | Districts (sample) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `flood_risk_district_high` | ≥ 20% | +25 | 2 | PE24, PE25 |
| `flood_risk_district_moderate` | 5.0% to < 20% | +10 | 73 | B6, B36, BD23, BN25, CA12, CA18, CA21, CA23, CB24, CR8, CT1, CT6, … (+61) |

## Caveats

- The file lists only postcodes with properties in or near areas at risk, so a district's share is measured over those postcodes, not over every home in the district: it overstates district-wide risk.
- Counts are residential properties only (home insurance), from the RES_* columns.
- District-level summaries are coarse: the Environment Agency states the data is not suitable for identifying whether an individual property will flood.
- Covers flooding from rivers and the sea in England only (not surface water, groundwater, Wales or Scotland).
- The synthetic book is made up; real impact depends on where actual customers live.
